Exposure-adjusted monitoring of flight cancellations for service-reliability governance: A Brazilian case study with a cross-country replication

Lucas Orbolato Carvalho · Davi Sombra Almeida · Daniel Alberto Pamplona

Publication

Case Studies on Transport Policy, Volume 26, Article 102001 (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.cstp.2026.102001. Open access (CC BY 4.0).

Publisher / DOIGitHub repositoryAuthor homepage

Study summary

Aviation authorities enforce passenger-rights obligations triggered by flight cancellations, yet usually monitor cancellations through counts and system-wide rates that confound program size with reliability. This case study examines whether a public flight registry can support exposure-adjusted monitoring, using the Active Regular Flight (VRA) registry of Brazil's National Civil Aviation Agency for 2015–2024, with the United States on-time performance registry as a replication. Regular flights are aggregated into operational cells by carrier group, origin airport, day type, and departure period, each with its cancellation rate. The Brazilian series is not homogeneous: the recorded monthly rate fell from 14.4% to 1.3% between February and April 2018, when a new registration system took effect, and the source of operated flights changed in April 2020. Cell rates persist: with lists fixed in the assignment year, the cells holding the tenth of scheduled exposure with the highest current rates had following-year rates 1.8 to 3.5 times that of all evaluated cells in Brazil and about twice that rate in the United States outside the 2020 shock year, while a three-segment K-Means partition added no consistent targeting power. The article proposes an annual monitoring protocol in which flagged cells trigger graduated review rather than findings of fault, with a second tier for low-frequency services and safeguards for the consistency of the registry.

Keywords: flight cancellations; service reliability; passenger rights; regulatory oversight; exposure normalization; registry comparability.

Code and reproducibility

The GitHub repository contains the analysis pipeline, the archived results behind every table, the figures, and checksum manifests of the public ANAC and BTS source files. All data tables of the article can be rebuilt from the archived results without downloading the source data.
