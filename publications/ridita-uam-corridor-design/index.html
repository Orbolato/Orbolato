Abstract

Integrating urban air mobility (UAM) into metropolitan airspace requires corridors respecting existing routes and airport flows. This paper presents a reproducible corridor-design procedure and dashboard for the São Paulo Metropolitan Region, applied to six- and twelve-vertiport networks on the Helicopter Special Routes (REH). Corridors 0.5 NM wide and 1,000 ft high, centered at 4,000 ft MSL between the REH and the Aircraft Special Routes (REA), are displaced 0.54–0.81 NM and lowered to 3,000 ft where they overlap. Volumetric overlap analysis shows the refinement lowered the corridor volume share inside other structures from 23.7% to 18.2%, mainly in the REA (8.1% to 3.1%). REH overlaps are thin (1.4%), but 14.0% lies in the Congonhas (SBSP) flow envelope derived from Flightradar24 records, largely from vertical allocation: the same paths in the REH have 2.8% of their centerline in that envelope and 0.3 SBSP movements a day crossing them, against 11.3% and 176 for the refined corridors. The 14.0% depends strongly on the envelope (2.0% for clustered streams), so overlap is read with observed traffic. The results are preliminary.

Method in brief

Network and demand

Initial and expanded networks connect six and twelve vertiports with three airports. Terminal pairs follow shortest paths on the REH graph; demand premises use 2023 Metrô origin–destination data and ANAC airport passenger information.

Corridor geometry

Two opposite-direction lanes have a nominal total width of 0.50 NM and a corridor height of 1,000 ft. The nominal axis is 4,000 ft MSL; refined segments may be displaced cross-track and lowered to 3,000 ft.

Airport-flow models

December 2022 Flightradar24 data support radial airport-flow envelopes and a clustered-stream alternative for SBSP and SBGR.

Verification

An analyst iterates visual 2D/3D checks and waypoint refinements. The dashboard quantifies residual volumetric intersections on a 50-m grid; observed flight tracks provide a separate exposure check.

Main findings

Refinement and residual overlap

The six-vertiport refinement displaced 16 waypoints (220 of 930 point records), with only 0.11% more track length. REA overlap fell from 8.1% to 3.1%; transverse REH crossings remained thin (1.4%).

In the twelve-vertiport network, total overlap fell from 24.9% to 19.6%.

Airspace and traffic exposure

For six vertiports, the refined corridor volume intersecting the radial SBSP envelope was 14.0%, versus 2.0% using clustered streams. The difference shows why envelope representation matters; individual narrow approach paths can carry significant traffic.

The REH reference uses the original undisplaced paths with their corresponding REH altitude bands and widths. It is not a controlled altitude-only comparison.

Interactive dashboard and reproducibility

The UAM Airspace Dashboard is the supporting tool described in the paper. It provides interactive 2D maps and 3D volume visualization, route editing, parallel-track generation, and automated volumetric overlap analysis. It is not an automated route-optimization system: offsets are chosen by the analyst.

Interpretation and limitations

A geometrically plausible corridor can still overlap heavily trafficked conventional aircraft paths. This study measures spatial intersections and historical traffic exposure, not simultaneous separation losses or certified operational safety.

Further validation must examine containment and RNP widths, the reduced vertical spacing, detailed terrain, operational procedures, and data coverage beyond one month of tracks.

Future work

The results motivate investigation of a hybrid network that retains dedicated corridors where suitable, avoids REA interference, and evaluates lower-altitude or REH-sharing alternatives near Congonhas under ATC coordination.

Next steps include geometry iteration and simulation in BlueSky for demand–capacity balancing and deconfliction.
